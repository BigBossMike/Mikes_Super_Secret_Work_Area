# Intro: Brisbane launch plan (draft)

## The idea

**Intro extends a digital hand in a modern world.** It's the introduction a friend used to make for you: "you should meet so-and-so, they're keen too." It should feel like putting your name down at the golf club and getting paired up.

Everyone has plans that need one more person: a fifth player, someone to see a film with, a midweek dinner, a walking buddy, a fourth for trivia. Intro is for everyone 16+ who'd rather do the thing than wait for the right person to turn up.

Two groups that never mix: 16 to 17, and 18+. Adults are only ever paired inside their chosen age range (default: their age plus or minus 10), and only if they're inside the other person's range too. A 55-year-old is never paired with a 19-year-old.

## How it should feel

- **Private by design.** Using Intro must never be something people can see or talk about. Nobody can browse or search it. You're only shown to someone you've been paired with and said yes to. Phone contacts, your school or workplace can be kept out. Notifications can be discreet. This is a big difference from dating apps, where anyone could see who was on them.
- **Confident and everyday.** Intro is a normal, smart way to get a plan going, like booking a court or a table. All messaging leads with the plan and the people, never with a problem.
- **Real life, not a feed.** No followers, likes, feed or public profiles. Nobody can see how many matches or connections anyone has.
- **Easy.** One plan a month, up to 5 people, the plan is clear before anyone agrees, and it's normal to bring a friend along to the first meet-up.

## Three audiences, three messages

| | Parents of teens (they find it, trust it, and probably pay) | Teens (they use it) | Adults (find it, use it and pay for themselves) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Message | "Help your teen find people to actually do things with, safely." | "Short a player? Want a movie buddy? Meet people near you who are keen too." | "Fancy a midweek dinner, a walk or a trivia night with someone new? Intro finds a few people near you who are keen too." |
| What they care about | Safety, ID checks, parent controls, real-world time instead of screen time | Not being awkward, not being judged, actually having something to do | Not a dating app, not a crowd, safe, and easy to say no to |
| Proof | Parent controls, every user ID-verified, minors never matched with adults, chats locked until in person | It's simple, it's not another social feed, their mates aren't watching | Everyone ID-verified, one clear plan, see who'll be there before you agree, meet somewhere public |

**Not a dating app.** Adults especially need to hear this. Keep plans activity-based and give a clear way to report anyone using it for dating.

**Pricing idea:** parents are likely to pay for teens, so consider a **Family plan** a parent buys for their teen, managed from the parent app. Adults pay for themselves on the normal plans.

## Why parents are the engine (and a way to reach adults too)

Mike's own parent network is the unfair advantage. Parents talk to other parents, sit on P&C committees, and run school and club group chats. A parent-led launch also builds trust that an app with teens on it needs.

Parents are adults too. Many will use it for their own plans, and they'll tell other adults about it.

## Phases

### Phase 0: now to end of term 4, 2026 (build and listen)

- Finish the app build, safety design and legal review (see README).
- **Talk to 20 parents and 20 teens** in your network before spending on ads. Ask: what would make you use it, what would make you nervous, what would you pay?
- Put up a **waitlist page** that asks for suburb and school (optional), so you know where people cluster.
- Recruit **10 to 20 "founding parents"** who'll each invite a few families.

### Phase 1: term 1, 2027 (invite-only pilot)

- **One small area**, for example 3 to 5 schools and a couple of sports clubs within about 5 km in inner-west Brisbane.
- **Run both groups in the same area:** teens through schools, clubs and parents, and adults through the same parents plus local community channels.
- **Don't start matching until there are enough people in a group.** Aim for roughly 150 to 300 verified people per group in the area first. Too few and matches are poor, and people leave.
- Free during the pilot.
- Founding parents host or suggest **easy first plans**: five-a-side, a movie night, a study group at the library.
- Weekly check-ins with founding parents. Fix what's awkward.

### Phase 2: terms 2 and 3, 2027 (Brisbane launch)

- Open suburb by suburb, following the waitlist.
- Turn on paid plans.
- Start paid social ads (below).
- Keep the 16 to 17 and 18+ groups completely separate, always.

## Channels

### Parent-first (start here)

- **Your parent network and founding parents:** word of mouth, school group chats, sideline conversations.
- **P&C associations and school newsletters:** a short, safety-led blurb. Offer a 20-minute talk at a P&C meeting.
- **Schools:** a one-page explainer for staff to pass to parents if they wish. Don't ask schools to "endorse" it early. Just inform.
- **Sports clubs:** "short a player?" is the perfect first plan. Clubs can share it with parents.
- **Brisbane parent Facebook groups:** posts from real parents, not ads.
- **Youth organisations** (PCYC, Scouts, church youth groups, community centres): possible partners once the pilot shows it's safe.

### Adult channels

- **Local spots:** cafés, gyms, libraries and community noticeboards. A poster with a QR code and one plan on it ("Midweek dinner, anyone?").
- **Local Facebook and suburb groups:** neighbourhood, interest and "new to Brisbane" groups.
- **Clubs and venues:** trivia nights, running clubs and social sport. They benefit when teams fill up.
- **Workplaces:** a simple way for staff to get plans going outside work.

### Paid social

- **Parents first:** Facebook and Instagram ads to parents in Brisbane suburbs on the waitlist. Lead with safety and real-world time.
- **Adults:** Facebook and Instagram ads in the same suburbs, led by the plan itself ("Midweek dinner, anyone?", "Walking buddy wanted").
- **Teens (16 to 17):** Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat ads, aimed at the pilot suburbs. Platforms limit how ads can be targeted at under-18s (usually age and location only), so keep the creative broad and lead with the activity, not the app.
- **Creative:** real Brisbane places (local pitches, the State Library, cinemas, cafés, the riverwalk), real-looking people of all ages out doing things. Feature "short a player", "movie buddy", "midweek dinner" and "walking buddy".
- **Start small.** Test a few ads in one suburb, measure waitlist sign-ups, then scale what works.

## What to measure

- **In-person connects per month.** This is the number that matters: people actually meeting.
- Share of plans that end in a connect.
- Share of people asked who say yes.
- Waitlist sign-ups per suburb, and verified users per area (the density threshold before switching matching on).
- Parent approval rate, and reports per 100 meet-ups. Safety has to be visibly good.
- Repeat use: do people set a second plan?

## Before any marketing starts

- Legal advice on the under-16 rules, privacy (especially ethnicity data), and terms of use for minors.
- Privacy policy and safety page written in plain English for parents.
- Check what checks people need in Queensland (for example a Blue Card) if staff or volunteers moderate or work with under-18s.
- A clear plan for handling reports quickly, with a named person responsible.
